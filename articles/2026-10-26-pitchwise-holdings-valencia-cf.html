US money lands at Mestalla.

Pitchwise Holdings × Valencia CF

FootballInvestmentGlobal
Photo: Unsplash

Valencia has sold 42% of the club to a US consortium with KKR's co-founders and the Denver Broncos' owners among its backers.

Pitchwise Holdings has agreed to buy a 42% stake in Valencia CF from Meriton Holdings, the company Peter Lim has used to own the club. The deal was announced on Friday 23 October. It values Valencia at €1.25 billion including debt, and investors will also put €100 million of new capital into the club. Meriton keeps majority control and Kiat Lim stays on as president.

Pitchwise is a consortium of family offices and long-term institutional investors, led by Ann Rodriguez and Corey Woolfolk, who join Lim in the new ownership group. The backers are the headline: KKR co-founders George Roberts and Henry Kravis, plus Penner Sports Group, the Denver investment business whose portfolio includes the NFL's Broncos.

Read the €1.25 billion carefully. It is an enterprise value, debt included, so it doesn't mean the 42% cost €525 million. The split between the share purchase and the €100 million injection hasn't been disclosed, so neither will we guess it.

What Valencia sold was a story about the future. The club says the valuation reflects its men's and women's teams, the 49,000-seat Mestalla and the Nou Mestalla project, planned to hold more than 70,000. That is a minority stake priced on a stadium that hasn't opened, and US money keeps showing it will pay for exactly that kind of upside in European football.

What to watch: regulatory approval and the other closing conditions, how quickly the new capital shows up at Nou Mestalla, and whether the new owners bring the American playbook with them, from naming rights to premium seating, once the stadium is built.

Valencia's new partners are betting on what Mestalla becomes.
