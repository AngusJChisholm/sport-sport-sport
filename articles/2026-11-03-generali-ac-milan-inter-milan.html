Milan's new home gets a name.

Generali × AC Milan / Inter Milan

FootballNaming rightsGlobal
Photo: Unsplash

San Siro's replacement has a name already, and it reportedly cost Generali about €650m.

AC Milan and Inter have unveiled the Generali Stadium, the 71,500-seat ground they plan to share in Milan, with the Italian insurer taking the naming rights and becoming Official Insurance Partner of both clubs. The clubs call it one of the largest stadium naming rights agreements in global sport. They haven't disclosed the terms.

The reported number is about €650m, carried by ESPN, Sportcal and Il Sole 24 Ore among others. The length is less settled. Most reports say 20 years, which works out at about €32.5m a year, while Il Sole 24 Ore says 25. Treat both as reported, not confirmed.

The timing is the interesting part. Construction isn't expected to start until 2028, and the first match is targeted for the 2031/32 season, subject to approvals. So Generali has put its name on a building that exists as a set of renders, around five years before anyone sits in it.

That's a different shape to most naming rights partnerships, where a brand buys into a venue fans can already see. Here the insurer gets in at the start, and the name is on the project from day one rather than arriving later to sit awkwardly beside an old one. Nobody will have to be talked out of calling the new place something else.

It also buys two fan bases at once. Most stadium names sit with one club and one set of supporters. This one covers both sides of one of the biggest rivalries in Europe, in the insurer's home market. The Official Insurance Partner role gives it a commercial reason to be in front of both clubs well before the building opens.

What to watch next is the approvals, because the 2031/32 target depends on them, and the partner line-up that follows. A name this size sets the tone for everything else the clubs try to sell around the building, and a shared stadium gives every other category the same two-for-one pitch.

Two rivals, one name on the door, and the first game still about five years off.
